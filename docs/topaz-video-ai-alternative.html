When Phoenix is the better pick

  • You'd rather own the software than rent it.

  • You're on Linux.
  • You want the restore to stay faithful to what was filmed, not invent a new face.

When Topaz is the better pick

  • You work on a Mac and want a desktop app. Phoenix doesn't have a macOS version yet.
  • You need frame interpolation, slow motion or stabilisation alongside upscaling.
  • You finish paid work and want a lot of manual control over every model.

If those are your jobs, Topaz earns its subscription. If the job is making old video watchable again, paying $299 every year for it is a lot of rent.

Questions

Is Topaz Video AI still a one-time purchase?

No. Topaz moved to subscriptions in 2025. Topaz Video is $59 a month or $299 a year, and the Topaz Studio bundle is $399 a year (topazlabs.com, September 2026). Older perpetual licences keep working but no longer get new models.

Is Phoenix Upscaler a subscription?

No. It's $129 once, every future update is included, and you can install it on up to three of your own computers.

Does Phoenix run on a Mac?

Not yet. On a Mac, use Cloud Restore: upload a clip, see the price in dollars, pay for that one clip, and download the result.

Is Phoenix as good as Topaz?

On old, low-resolution footage, judge it on your own clip. The free download is there so you can compare the two side by side before paying anything. Phoenix aims for a clean, faithful picture rather than invented detail.
