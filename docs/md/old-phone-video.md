# How to make old phone videos look better

Find the original file, not a copy that went through a chat app. Clean up the compression and noise with a restoration model instead of a sharpening filter. Then upscale it once, to 1080p. Most clips from the early 2010s come out clean and steady enough for a modern screen, and faces that filled the frame improve the most.

Updated 28 September 2026

## Why old phone videos look so bad now

They looked fine on the phone that shot them. Four things have caught up with them since.

- **They're small.** Plenty of phones from before about 2012 filmed at 480p or 720p, and front cameras at less. That's a small picture to spread across a 4K TV.
- **They're heavily compressed.** To save space, phones threw away most of the detail as they recorded. It shows up as blocks in dark areas and smeared faces whenever anything moves.
- **The sensors were tiny.** In poor light a phone sensor makes noise, and the compression turns that noise into crawling mush.
- **Copies of copies.** Every trip through a messaging app or a social site compresses the clip again, and the damage stacks.

## 1. Find the best copy you have

This matters more than any setting. Look wherever the original might still be:

- the old phone itself, or its memory card;
- a computer you synced or backed the phone up to;
- a cloud photo backup, if it kept the original quality. Space-saving settings compress videos again.

If you find several copies at the same resolution, the bigger file is usually the less compressed one. Anything saved out of WhatsApp, Messenger or a social network has been compressed at least once more.

## 2. Restore it instead of sharpening it

The sharpen and “enhance” sliders in phone and photo apps can't tell compression blocks from real detail, so they make both crisper. A restoration model has learned what compression and noise look like and rebuilds the detail underneath instead. Go easy on separate noise reduction first: the model works from that texture, and a scrubbed clip comes out waxy.

## 3. Upscale once, to the size you'll watch

1080p suits most screens and is a comfortable step up from 480p or 720p. 4K is worth it for a clean 720p or better clip you'll watch on a big TV. Do it in the same pass as the restore: upscaling, then restoring, then upscaling again stacks each step's artifacts.

## 4. Keep vertical clips vertical

A lot of phone video from the 2010s is portrait. Restore it as it is. A crop to widescreen or blurred side bars are choices for when you put it on a TV, and easy to add afterwards, but a crop throws away picture you can't get back.

## 5. Test 30 seconds first

Pick a stretch with a face, some movement and a dark corner, restore just that, and watch it full screen. If it looks natural, run the whole clip with the same settings.

## What restoration can and can't do for a phone clip

It can clean up compression blocks, noise and softness, and give you a steady picture at a size that holds up on a big screen. It can't sharpen a shot that was out of focus, undo heavy motion blur, or bring back a face that was a handful of pixels across. Clips shot in daylight improve the most. Very dark ones improve less, because there was little real detail under the noise.

## Questions

### Can AI fix a blurry old phone video?

Partly. Blur from low resolution and compression is exactly what restoration models are good at, and that's most of what makes old phone clips look soft. Blur from missed focus or a shaky hand in low light can only be reduced a little.

### What resolution should I upscale an old phone video to?

1080p for most clips. 4K is worth it when the original is 720p or better, fairly clean, and you'll watch it on a big TV. Going much further mostly makes bigger files.

### Why does my video look worse after sending it on WhatsApp?

Messaging apps compress videos again to save data, on top of the compression the phone already did. Restore from the original file on the phone or your computer if you still have it, not the copy from the chat.

### Can I improve an old video on my phone?

Phone editing apps can brighten a clip and sharpen it a little, but that sharpens the damage too.
